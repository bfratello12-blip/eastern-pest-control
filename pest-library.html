Pest library

Identifying the species correctly is most of the job. Here's what we see most across New Jersey, New York and eastern Pennsylvania — what it looks like, what it costs you and how we handle it.

Ants

Pavement, odorous house and pharaoh ants are the region's big three. A visible trail is only about 10% of the colony.

Signs
Trails along baseboards, sand piles at slab cracks, winged swarmers at windows
Risk
Food contamination; pharaoh ants spread when treated incorrectly
Season
March – October
We use
Non-repellent baits carried back to the queen
Get treatment

Carpenter Ants

Large black ants that excavate galleries in damp structural wood. They don't eat it — they hollow it out.

Signs
Coarse sawdust (frass), rustling in walls at night, large winged ants indoors
Risk
Structural damage to sills, headers and rafters over time
Season
April – September
We use
Void dusts, exterior perimeter and moisture correction
Get treatment

Bed Bugs

Hitchhikers, not a hygiene problem. They arrive in luggage, used furniture and shared laundry, then hide within eight feet of where you sleep.

Signs
Rust-coloured spotting on seams, shed skins, bites in clustered lines
Risk
Sleep loss, secondary infection, rapid spread between units
Season
Year-round
We use
Canine inspection, whole-room heat or conventional treatment
Get treatment

Cockroaches

German roaches breed fastest and hide in warm voids — motor housings, hinge gaps, under the coffee maker. American roaches come up from drains and sewers.

Signs
Pepper-like droppings, egg cases in cabinet corners, a musty odour
Risk
Asthma triggers, salmonella transfer, health code violations
Season
Year-round
We use
Rotating gel baits plus insect growth regulators
Get treatment

Termites

Eastern subterranean termites work from the soil up and can go years without visible evidence above grade.

Signs
Pencil-width mud tubes, hollow-sounding trim, discarded wings on sills
Risk
Serious structural damage; almost never covered by homeowners insurance
Season
Swarms March – May; activity year-round
We use
Liquid soil barriers or in-ground bait systems, monitored annually
Get treatment

Mice & Rats

A house mouse fits through a gap the width of a pencil; a Norway rat needs about the size of a quarter. Both find one on almost every older building.

Signs
Droppings along walls, gnaw marks, grease rub marks, scratching overhead at night
Risk
Chewed wiring and fire risk, contaminated food, hantavirus and salmonella
Season
Peak September – February
We use
Interior trapping plus permanent physical exclusion
Get treatment

Spiders

Almost every spider in this region is harmless and is actually a sign of something else — they follow the insects they eat.

Signs
Webs in corners, eaves and window wells; egg sacs in basements
Risk
Low; mainly a nuisance and an indicator of other pest activity
Season
Peak August – October
We use
De-webbing, exterior barrier and reduction of their food source
Get treatment

Mosquitoes

Asian tiger mosquitoes breed in a bottle cap of water and bite during the day, which is why they've taken over yards across the region.

Signs
Daytime biting, activity concentrated in shade and dense plantings
Risk
West Nile virus, EEE; misery in your own backyard
Season
April – October
We use
Monthly barrier treatment plus larvicide in standing water
Get treatment

Ticks & Fleas

Deer ticks live along the transition between lawn and woods. Fleas usually arrive on a pet and end up in the carpet.

Signs
Ticks found on pets or clothing; pets scratching, flea dirt in bedding
Risk
Lyme disease, anaplasmosis, babesiosis; flea allergy dermatitis in pets
Season
April – November
We use
Perimeter granular, tick tubes, interior IGR treatment for fleas
Get treatment

Wasps & Hornets

Yellow jacket colonies peak in late summer at several thousand workers, and they get noticeably more aggressive as food sources dry up.

Signs
Steady traffic to one point in a soffit, wall or the ground; visible paper nests
Risk
Painful repeat stings; serious danger for anyone with an allergy
Season
May – October, worst in August and September
We use
Direct nest treatment, physical removal and entry sealing
Get treatment

Stink Bugs & Cluster Flies

Overwintering invaders. They gather on sunny south-facing walls in fall, then squeeze into attic and wall voids to wait out the cold.

Signs
Dozens on siding in October; insects appearing indoors on warm winter days
Risk
Nuisance and odour; staining on light-coloured surfaces
Season
September – April
We use
Timed fall exterior treatment before they enter, plus sealing
Get treatment

Silverfish & Earwigs

Moisture pests. Where you find them, there's almost always a humidity or drainage problem behind the wall.

Signs
Insects in tubs, sinks and basements; irregular holes in stored paper or fabric
Risk
Damage to books, wallpaper and stored textiles
Season
Year-round, worse in humid months
We use
Void dusts, targeted crack treatment and moisture recommendations
Get treatment

Flies

Small flies are a sanitation puzzle, not a spraying problem. Drain, fruit and phorid flies each point to a different source.

Signs
Persistent small flies near drains, mops, produce or a slab crack
Risk
Pathogen transfer; a common health inspection finding
Season
Year-round indoors
We use
Source location, drain biofoam, and light trap placement
Get treatment

Pantry Pests

Indian meal moths and grain beetles usually come home in a bag of flour, birdseed or dry pet food and spread from there.

Signs
Webbing in dry goods, small moths flying in the kitchen, larvae on ceilings
Risk
Contaminated food and repeated re-infestation from an unfound source
Season
Year-round
We use
Source removal, shelf treatment and pheromone monitoring
Get treatment

Wildlife

Squirrels, raccoons, bats and opossums enter through roof returns, ridge vents, chimney chases and rotted fascia.

Signs
Heavy movement overhead, chewed vent screens, staining below entry points, odour
Risk
Chewed wiring, ruined insulation, rabies and histoplasmosis exposure
Season
Peak in spring birthing season and late fall
We use
One-way doors, humane removal, steel exclusion and attic restoration
Get treatment

Birds

Pigeons, starlings and sparrows roosting on ledges, signage and rooftop equipment — mostly a commercial problem, and a corrosive one.

Signs
Droppings below ledges, nesting material in equipment, persistent noise
Risk
Corrosion, slip hazards, histoplasmosis, blocked drainage
Season
Year-round
We use
Netting, spike and wire systems, plus clean-up and sanitizing
Get treatment
